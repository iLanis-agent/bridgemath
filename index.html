Bridge Math

With a 5 V excitation, one ohm of imbalance across 1000 Ω arms reads 1.25 mV.

Boundary: arithmetic only Bridge Math works out Wheatstone bridge leg voltages, readings, unknown arms and per-ohm shifts from the numbers you type, treating each leg as an ideal divider. It has not seen your bridge and does not know the wiring, the temperature drift, the excitation stability or the sensor condition, and it gives no verdict that a measurement circuit is adequate or safe. Every number here is your own entry.

Starting examples: a 5 V bridge with one arm at 1001 Ω, an unknown worked out from a 1010 Ω known arm, and the shift per ohm around 1000 Ω.

The reading truth

With a 5 V excitation, the left leg sits at 2500 mV and the right leg at 2501.25 mV, so the bridge reads 1.25 mV.

The unknown truth

With 1000 Ω and 1000 Ω on one side and a 1010 Ω known arm in balance, the unknown works out to 1010 Ω.

The shift truth

With a 5 V excitation and 1000 Ω arms, a 1 Ω shift around 1000 Ω moves the reading by 1.25 mV.
